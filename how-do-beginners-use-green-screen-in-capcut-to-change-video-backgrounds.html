How Do Beginners Use Green Screen in CapCut to Change Video Backgrounds?

Video Editing · 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You use green screen in CapCut by filming yourself in front of a flat green cloth with even light, then you remove the green with the chroma key tool. You place a photo or a video behind your clip, and you adjust the strength until the edges look clean. You then export the video in the same quality you filmed it in.

You change a video background in CapCut by filming in front of a green cloth and removing the green with one tool. The cloth turns into any place you pick, from a beach to a clean studio wall.

Most beginners film against a wrinkled cloth in dim light, and the edges glow green around their hair. This habit breaks the effect, because the tool cannot tell the cloth from the person.

You will finish this guide with a clean video that looks filmed on location. You will know how to light the cloth, cut the background, and export the final clip.

Set Up the Green Screen and the Light First

Learning how to use green screen in CapCut for beginners starts before you press record, because the cloth and the light decide the final quality. You hang the cloth flat with no folds, and you light it evenly from both sides. The even light gives the tool one clean colour to remove, and the flat cloth gives it no shadows to confuse it.

Hang the cloth flat with no folds

You hang the green cloth on a wall or a stand, and you pull it tight so no fold or crease shows. A fold casts a shadow, and the shadow turns into a dark patch the tool reads as a different colour. You iron the cloth when it arrives wrinkled, and you tape the bottom edge to the floor so it stays flat.

Light the screen and your face separately

You place one light on each side of the cloth, and you point both lights at the cloth and not at your face. The two lights wash the cloth in even brightness, and they leave no dark corner behind you. You then light your face with a third lamp from the front, so your face stays bright and clear.

Film Your Clip With Room to Cut

The film step sets the raw material for the edit, so you leave room at each end of the clip. Learning how to use green screen in CapCut for beginners means you film a few extra seconds before and after each take, because the trim needs that spare footage. You stand a step away from the cloth, and you frame the shot with space above your head.

Stand a step away from the screen

You stand a full step away from the cloth, because your body casts a shadow on the cloth when you stand too close. The shadow darkens the green behind you, and the tool then leaves a green patch around your shoulders. A creator in Lahore moved one step back after a green glow ruined her first video, and the next take cut clean.

Record a few extra seconds at each end

You record three seconds before you start speaking, and you hold the pose for three seconds after you finish. The spare seconds give the editor a clean place to cut in and out, and they save a take that starts too tight. Remove the Background With Chroma Key in CapCut

Chroma key is the tool that removes the green, and CapCut puts it one tap away in the edit screen. Learning how to use green screen in CapCut for beginners means you add your clip to the timeline, open the chroma key tool, and tap the green cloth to sample its colour. The tool then turns that colour clear, and your face and clothes stay in place.

Add the clip and open the chroma key tool

You open CapCut and start a new project, then you add the green screen clip to the timeline. Learning how to use green screen in CapCut for beginners feels simple at this point, because you tap the clip to select it, and you scroll the bottom menu until you find the chroma key button. The tool opens with a colour picker, and you tap the green cloth in the preview to sample it.

Adjust the strength until the edges look clean

You drag the strength slider up a little at a time, and you watch the edges of your hair and shoulders after each move. A low strength leaves green patches, while a high strength eats into your clothes and your hair. You stop at the point where the green is gone and your edges still look full, and you zoom the preview to check the fine hairs.

Place the New Background and Export the Video

The new background turns the empty space into a place, so you pick an image or a video that fits the story. Learning how to use green screen in CapCut for beginners ends with a background layer under your clip, and the layer must sit below your video on the timeline. You add the background as an overlay track, and you stretch it to match the length of your clip.

Add a photo or video behind your clip

You tap the overlay button in CapCut, and you add a photo or a video as the background layer. You drag the layer below your main clip on the timeline, so the background sits behind you and not in front. A still photo suits a lesson, while a moving video suits a travel story, and B-roll adds the same depth to any edit, so read How Do Beginners Use B Roll To Make Videos Look Professional after you place the background.

Export in the same quality you filmed in

You export the video at the same resolution and frame rate you filmed it in, because a mismatch softens the image. CapCut shows the export settings on the final screen, and you match them to the clip settings. You play the exported file once from start to end, and you check the edges around your hair in the final version.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is chroma key in CapCut?

Chroma key is the tool in CapCut that removes one colour from a video, and you use it to take out a green screen. You tap the green cloth to sample its colour, and the tool turns that colour clear. You then place a photo or a video behind your clip, and the scene looks like one shot.

Do I need a real green screen cloth for CapCut?

A plain green cloth works best, because CapCut needs one even colour to remove cleanly. You can also use a green wall or a large green sheet when the colour stays flat and even. A wrinkled cloth or a mixed colour makes the removal harder, so the cloth is worth the small cost.

Why does green glow stay around my hair after chroma key?

A green glow stays when the cloth sits too close or the light falls unevenly, because the tool then reads the edge as a mix of green and hair. You move a step back from the cloth, and you light the cloth evenly from both sides. You also lower the strength slider a little, so the tool stops eating into your hair.

Can I use green screen in the free version of CapCut?

You can use chroma key in the free version of CapCut, because the tool sits in the basic edit menu. You open a new project, add your clip, and tap the chroma key button in the bottom menu. The free version covers the full green screen job, and you pay only for extra effects.

What colour works best for a green screen background?

A bright even green works best, because cameras read green cleanly and the tool removes it with ease. You avoid clothes in the same green, or the tool will remove parts of your shirt too. A blue screen works as a second choice, and it suits a subject with green clothes.

How do I light a green screen with cheap lamps?

You place one lamp on each side of the cloth, and you point both lamps at the cloth and not at your face. You add a third lamp in front for your face, so you stay bright and clear. The even light on the cloth matters more than the price of the lamps, and two household lamps do the job.

Can I change the background after I export the video?

You change the background by opening the project again in CapCut and swapping the background layer. You keep the project saved, so the swap takes only a few taps. You export the video again after the swap, and the new background takes the place of the old one.

Why does my green screen video look fake?

A fake look comes from a mismatch between you and the background, such as light from a different side or a background that moves the wrong way. You match the light direction, and you pick a background that fits the scale of your shot. The small match in light and scale makes the scene feel like one real place.
