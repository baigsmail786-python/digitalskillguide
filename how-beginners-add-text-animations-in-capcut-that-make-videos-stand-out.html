How Beginners Add Text Animations in CapCut That Make Videos Stand Out

Video Editing · 21 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You add a text animation in CapCut by tapping the text clip, opening the animation menu, and picking an in animation and an out animation. You set the speed so the words match your voice, and you preview the clip before you export.

Most beginners pick the flashiest animation for every line, and the video turns into a noisy show that tires the eye. This habit hides the message, because the motion wins the attention the words need.

You will finish this guide with text that moves with your voice. You will know which style fits each line, and you will export a video that holds the viewer.

Add Text and Pick a Style That Fits

Learning how to add text animations in CapCut for beginners starts with the text itself, because the words carry the message and the motion only lifts them. You tap Text in CapCut, type your line, and pick a font that reads clear. You place the text where the eye lands first, and you keep it away from the edges. The clear text sets the base, and the animation then adds the life.

Type the text and pick a clear font

You tap the Text button, type your line in short words, and pick a bold font that reads clear at a small size. The bold font holds its shape on a phone screen, and the short line fits the frame. You keep the line to five words or fewer, because a long line crowds the video. A creator in Lahore used short bold lines, and her viewers read each one in time.

Place the text where the eye lands

You drag the text to the middle of the frame, because the eye lands there first on a phone screen. You keep it clear of faces and hands, and you leave space above and below it. The clear spot lets the words breathe, and the viewer reads them without a search. You test the spot on a small preview, and you move the text when it hides the face.

Set the In and Out Animation

Learning how to add text animations in CapCut for beginners moves to the animation menu next, because CapCut splits each animation into an in, an out, and a loop. You tap the text clip, open Animations, and pick an in animation that brings the words on screen. You then pick an out animation that takes them off with the same calm. The pair gives the text a clean start and end, and the viewer never sees a hard cut.

Pick an in animation that fits the mood

You open the In tab, and you test three styles on the same line before you pick one. A fade suits a calm lesson, a slide suits a fast tip, and a pop suits a fun moment. You watch each test on the phone screen, and you pick the one that feels right for the line. The right in animation pulls the eye to the words, and it sets the mood.

Match the out animation to the in one

You open the Out tab, and you pick an out animation that mirrors the in one you chose. A fade in pairs with a fade out, and a slide in pairs with a slide out, so the text leaves the way it came. The match keeps the clip calm, and the viewer feels the polish. You never mix a pop in with a slow fade out, because the clash breaks the flow.

Time the Text to Your Voice

Learning how to add text animations in CapCut for beginners moves to timing next, because text that lands late or leaves early fights the voice. You drag the text clip until it starts with the word you speak, and you stretch it until the line ends. You play the clip back with sound, and you nudge it until the words and the voice move as one. The tight timing makes the video feel pro, and the viewer stays to the end.

Split long lines into short clips

You split a long sentence into two or three short text clips, and you place each one where its words sound. The short clips keep the screen clean, and the viewer reads each line in time. You never show a full paragraph at once, because the wall of words hides the face. A teacher in Multan split her lesson into short clips, and her students watched it all.

Set the speed so the words match your voice

You open the animation settings, and you drag the speed slider until the words move at the pace you speak. A slow speed suits a lesson, and a fast speed suits a quick tip. You test the speed on one line first, and you adjust it until the motion feels calm. The right speed keeps the words with the voice, and the viewer never reads ahead of you.

Use Templates and Effects With Care

Learning how to add text animations in CapCut for beginners ends with templates, because a template gives you a tested style in one tap. You open the Text templates tab, and you preview three styles. You pick the style that fits your video, and you change the words and the colours to your own. The template saves you time, and it keeps your videos looking steady from one post to the next.

Use the typewriter effect for lessons

Learning how to add text animations in CapCut for beginners gets easier with the typewriter effect, because it shows one letter at a time. You pick the typewriter style from the In tab, and you set the speed to match your voice. The letters appear as you speak them, and the viewer reads with you. Keep one style per video

You pick one text style for the whole video, and you use it for every line. The single style gives the video a clean look, and the viewer learns where to read. You never mix a typewriter line with a pop line, because the mix tires the eye. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best text animation in CapCut for beginners?

A fade or a slide works best for beginners, because both styles read clear and suit most videos. You test each one on your own line, and you pick the style that fits the mood of the clip. You keep the same style through the video, because one style looks cleaner than three.

How do I add a typewriter effect in CapCut?

You tap the text clip, open Animations, and pick the typewriter style from the In tab. You set the speed to match your voice, and you play the clip back to check the pace. The letters then appear one at a time as you speak, and the viewer reads with you.

Can I use CapCut text animations for free?

You can use most text animations in CapCut for free, because the free plan covers the core In, Out, and Loop styles. You tap any style to test it, and CapCut marks the paid ones with a small pro tag. You build the whole video with free styles, and you pay only when a pro style truly fits.

How do I put text behind a person in CapCut?

You place the text on the timeline, and you use the overlay and mask tools to hide the part that sits in front of the person. You set the mask around the person, and the text then shows only behind them. You play the clip back frame by frame, and you fix the mask when the edge slips.

Why does my CapCut text move too fast?

Your text moves too fast when the animation speed sits high or the clip runs too short for the words. You drag the speed slider down, and you stretch the text clip until each word gets time to land. You play the clip back with sound, and you adjust the speed until the words match your voice.

What font size works best for Reels text?

A large bold font works best for Reels, because most viewers watch on a phone and read at a glance. You test the text on your own phone, and you grow the size until it reads clear from arm length. You keep the line short, because a large font needs room to breathe.

Can I save a text style in CapCut to reuse it?

You save a text style by copying a clip that holds it, and you paste the clip into your next project. You change only the words, and the font, colour, and animation stay the same. You keep one project as a style bank, and you copy from it each time you start a new video.

How do I add captions that move with my voice in CapCut?

You open the Captions tool in CapCut, and it turns your speech into timed text on the timeline. You check each line for a wrong word, and you fix the spelling before you style it. You then pick one animation for all the captions, and the words move with your voice through the video.
