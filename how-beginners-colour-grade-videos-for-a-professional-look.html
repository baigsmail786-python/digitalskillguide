How Beginners Colour Grade Videos for a Professional Look

Video Editing · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Colour grade videos like a pro: fix exposure and white balance first, then shape the look with contrast, saturation and LUTs in any free editor.

Colour grading gives your video a clean and polished look that viewers notice at once. You fix the light first, then you shape the colours to match the mood. This guide shows you how to colour grade videos for beginners step by step.

Most beginners push the saturation slider to the top and add a heavy filter. The skin turns orange, the sky turns blue and the video looks fake. They fail because they chase a look instead of fixing the shot first.

After this guide you will grade your videos with a calm hand and a clear order.

Learn the Difference Between Correction and Grading

You start by learning the difference between correction and grading. Correction fixes the shot so it looks natural and true to life. You learn how to colour grade videos for beginners by fixing first and styling second, never the other way round.

You correct exposure, white balance and contrast so the shot looks clean. You grade after that by shaping the mood with warmer or cooler tones. You keep the two steps apart so a style choice never hides a real problem. You check the corrected shot on a bright screen before you add any style.

A creator in Lahore films a street food video that looks too yellow. She fixes the white balance first and the food looks natural again. She adds a warm grade after that and the video feels inviting. You can follow the same order and your grades will sit on a clean base.

Fix Exposure and White Balance First

You fix exposure first so the bright and dark parts of the shot sit right. You raise the exposure a little when the shot looks dark and flat. You lower it when the sky turns white and loses detail. You watch the skin tones because they show exposure errors first.

You fix white balance next so white objects look white and not yellow or blue. You use the eyedropper tool on a white wall or a white shirt in the shot. You warm the shot slightly when it looks cold and clinical. You check the fix on a phone screen because most viewers watch there.

A student in Karachi films a classroom video that looks blue and cold. She warms the white balance a little and the skin tones turn natural. She raises the exposure slightly and the room feels bright. You can fix exposure and balance the same way before you touch any style.

Shape the Look With Contrast and Saturation

You shape the look with contrast and saturation after the fix. You raise contrast a little so the darks feel deep and the lights feel bright. You learn how to colour grade videos for beginners by moving one slider at a time and watching the result.

You raise saturation gently so colours feel rich without turning fake. You lower saturation when skin tones start to look orange or red. You add a little vibrance instead when your editor offers it, because it lifts dull colours and spares the skin. You compare the graded shot with the original side by side.

A beginner in Faisalabad grades a travel video and pushes the saturation too far. He pulls it back a little and the sky turns soft blue again. He adds a touch of contrast and the streets gain depth. You can move one slider at a time the same way and stop when the shot looks real.

Try LUTs and Presets With a Light Hand

You can try a LUT when you want a ready made look without building it yourself. A LUT shifts the colours of your shot toward one style, such as warm film or cool teal. You apply it at half strength first so the look stays gentle. You adjust exposure again after the LUT because it often shifts the light.

You pick a LUT that suits the mood of your video, not the trend of the week. You test it on one clip before you apply it to the whole video. You lower its strength when skin tones start to look strange. You save your own preset when you find a mix that you like.

A creator in Multan applies a warm film LUT to a wedding video at full strength. The skin turns orange, so she lowers the LUT to half strength. The video gains a soft golden feel and the skin looks natural. You can test a LUT the same way and keep its strength low until the look feels right.

Match Your Shots and Export With Care

You match your shots so the whole video feels like one piece. You copy the grade from one clip to the next clip in the same scene. You adjust each clip slightly because the light changes between shots. You watch the full video once to spot any clip that stands out.

You export the video in the right size for the platform where it will play. You pick 1080p for a sharp result on most phones. You watch the exported file from start to end before you share it. You fix any clip that looks off and you export the video again.

A student in Islamabad grades a short film with five clips from one evening shoot. She copies the grade across the clips and tweaks each one for the light. She exports the film and watches it fully on her phone. You can match and export the same way and your video will feel polished.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is colour grading in simple words?

Colour grading means shaping the colours of a video to match a mood or a style. You fix the light first and then you adjust contrast, saturation and tone. You can think of it as painting over a clean photo with a clear style in mind.

What is the difference between colour correction and colour grading?

Correction fixes the shot so it looks natural and true to life. Grading shapes the mood after the fix, such as warm tones for a happy scene. You should always correct first and grade second.

Do I need paid software to colour grade videos?

No, free tools such as DaVinci Resolve and CapCut offer strong colour tools at no cost. You can fix exposure, balance and saturation inside these free editors. You should pick one tool and learn its colour panel well.

How can beginners practise colour grading?

You learn how to colour grade videos for beginners by fixing one clip each day and comparing it with the original. You should move one slider at a time and watch the result. You should keep a note of the settings that gave you the best look.

What is a LUT in video editing?

A LUT is a ready made colour preset that shifts your shot toward one style. You apply it at half strength first so the look stays gentle. You should adjust exposure again after the LUT because it often shifts the light.

Why does my graded video look fake?

Your video can look fake when the saturation runs too high or the skin turns orange. You should pull the sliders back a little and compare the shot with the original. You should stop when the shot looks real and the mood still shows.

Can I colour grade videos on my phone?

Yes, apps such as CapCut let you adjust brightness, contrast and saturation on your phone. You should work on a bright screen and check the result on another phone too. You should keep the grade gentle because small screens hide small errors.

How do I match colours across different clips?

You should copy the grade from one clip to the next clip in the same scene. You should adjust each clip slightly because the light changes between shots. You should watch the full video once to spot any clip that stands out.
