How Beginners Run Their First Google Ads Campaign on a Small Budget

Digital Marketing · 20 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You start Google Ads by opening an account in Expert Mode, picking one clear goal, and setting a small daily budget you can afford. You choose a short list of keywords that match real searches, write two ads, and watch the clicks for a few days. You then keep the ads that earn clicks and pause the ones that do not.

You run your first Google Ads campaign by setting one goal, one small budget, and one short list of keywords. The campaign shows your ad to people who search for what you sell, and you pay only when someone clicks.

Most beginners turn on every setting Google suggests, and the budget drains on broad searches that never buy. This habit burns the budget fast, because the ad reaches people who only browse.

You will finish this guide with a live campaign that spends a little each day. You will know how to pick keywords, write the ad, and read the first results.

Set Up Your Account in Expert Mode

Learning how to start Google Ads for beginners begins with Expert Mode, because the simple setup hides the choices that shape your budget. Expert Mode shows you the goal, the network, and the bidding in plain steps, and you control each one. You open the Google Ads site, sign in with your Google account, and choose Expert Mode when the setup asks. The choice takes a minute, and it gives you full control from the first day.

Skip the smart setup and choose Expert Mode

You skip the smart setup when Google offers it, because that setup picks broad keywords and a high budget for you. Expert Mode asks you the same questions one by one, and you answer each with your own numbers. The slower setup teaches you the parts of a campaign, and it stops Google from spending your budget on guesses.

Set your billing country and currency first

You set the country and the currency before you build anything, because Google locks those choices once the account runs. The currency must match the card you pay with, or the bank may block the payment. A bakery in Faisalabad set its account in rupees from the start, and its first ad ran the same evening without a payment fault.

Pick One Clear Goal and One Small Budget

A campaign with one goal spends its budget on one result, while a campaign with three goals spends it on none. Learning how to start Google Ads for beginners means you pick the goal that matches your business, such as calls, visits, or sales. You then set a daily budget that you can afford to spend for two weeks without worry.

Choose the goal that matches your business

You pick website traffic when you want visitors, and you pick calls when you want the phone to ring. The goal tells Google what a good click looks like, and Google shows your ad to people who act that way. A shop that wants footfall picks store visits, while an online course picks signups.

Start with a daily budget you can afford to lose

You set a daily budget that feels small, because the first campaign teaches you more than it earns. The budget caps the spend each day, and Google stops showing the ad once the cap is met. The same small budget habit works on other platforms, so read How Beginners Run Their First Facebook Ad With A Small Budget when you want to test social ads next.

Choose Keywords That Match Real Searches

Keywords decide who sees your ad, so you pick phrases that real buyers type and not phrases that sound clever. Learning how to start Google Ads for beginners means you start with a short list of exact phrases, and you grow the list only after the first clicks arrive. You type each phrase into Google first, and you read the ads and results that appear.

Start with a short list of exact phrases

You list ten phrases that name your service and your city, such as plumber in Lahore or best biryani in Karachi. Learning how to start Google Ads for beginners feels simple at this point, because the short list keeps the budget tight, and each phrase earns enough clicks to judge it. You add the phrases as exact match first, so Google shows the ad only for close searches.

Add negative keywords from day one

You add negative keywords to block the searches you never want, such as free, jobs, or course when you sell a service. The negative list stops your ad from showing to people who want free help or a job, and it saves the budget for real buyers. You check the search terms report after a few days, and you add each wasted search to the negative list. The list grows with each check, and the campaign gets sharper every week.

Write the Ad and Watch the First Results

The ad text decides the click, so you write it for one reader with one clear benefit. Learning how to start Google Ads for beginners ends with two ads that test each other, because the clicks show you which line works. You write a headline that names the benefit, and you add a second line that proves it. The reader then sees the gain at once, and the click feels like the next step.

Write two headlines that name the benefit

You write two headlines that lead with the result the reader wants, such as fresh bread at your door before seven. The benefit pulls the reader in, and the second line adds the proof, such as baked each morning in your city. You test the two headlines against each other, and you keep the one that earns more clicks. Strong ad copy follows the same benefit first rule, so read How Do Beginners Write Ad Copy That Gets Clicks On Facebook And Instagram for the full method.

Check the results after three days and adjust

You wait three days before you judge the campaign, because a single day of clicks says too little. You read the click rate and the cost per click, and you pause the ad that earns few clicks at a high cost. You then raise the budget a little on the ad that wins, and you add its best phrase to the keyword list. The small weekly check keeps the campaign honest, and it stops a weak ad from eating the budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to start Google Ads?

You start with a small daily budget that you can afford for two weeks, because the first campaign teaches you more than it earns. The budget caps the spend each day, and Google stops the ad once the cap is met. You raise the budget only on the ads that earn clicks at a fair cost.

What is Expert Mode in Google Ads?

Expert Mode is the full setup path in Google Ads, and it shows you the goal, the network, and the bidding in plain steps. The simple setup hides those choices and picks broad settings for you. You choose Expert Mode at the start, so you control the budget and the keywords from day one.

How do I pick keywords for my first campaign?

You pick short phrases that name your service and your city, and you type each phrase into Google first. The search page shows you the ads and results that appear, and it tells you whether buyers or browsers search that phrase. You start with ten exact phrases, and you grow the list after the first clicks arrive.

What are negative keywords in Google Ads?

Negative keywords block the searches you never want, such as free or jobs when you sell a service. You add them to the campaign, and Google stops showing your ad for those searches. You check the search terms report each week, and you add each wasted search to the list.

How long before Google Ads shows results?

You wait three days before you judge a new campaign, because a single day of clicks says too little. You read the click rate and the cost per click, and you pause the weak ads. The campaign sharpens each week as you add negative keywords and raise the budget on winners.

Can I run Google Ads without a website?

You can run ads that send people to a call or a Google Business Profile, but a simple landing page works better for most goals. The page must match the ad promise, or visitors leave at once. You build one clear page for the offer before you spend on clicks.

Why does Google Ads cost so much per click?

The cost per click follows the competition for each phrase, and popular phrases cost more than narrow ones. You lower the cost by picking longer phrases with less competition, and by adding negative keywords. A tight keyword list and a clear ad also lift the quality score, and that lowers the price.

Should I run Google Ads and Facebook Ads together?

You can run both, because Google catches people who search and Facebook catches people who scroll. You start with one platform first, and you learn its numbers before you add the second. The small budget habit works on both, and you keep the platform that earns the cheaper clicks.
