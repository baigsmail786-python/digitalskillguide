How Beginners Edit Videos in DaVinci Resolve for Free

Video Editing · 13 November 2026 · 7 min read

Edit videos in DaVinci Resolve for free as a beginner. This guide covers download, the Edit page, timeline basics, colour, and export.

You edit videos in DaVinci Resolve by importing clips, cutting them on the timeline, and exporting the finished video for free. You learn how to edit videos in DaVinci Resolve for beginners with a simple order that keeps every step small.

Most beginners open every page at once and they feel lost in the tools. They add many effects before they learn the basic cut.

You finish this guide with a clean first video that you edited and exported yourself. You gain confidence to edit the next video faster and you keep improving with each project.

Download the Free Version and Open Your First Project

You start when you learn how to edit videos in DaVinci Resolve for beginners by downloading the DaVinci Resolve free version for beginners from the official site. You install it with the standard options and you open it once to check that it runs. You create a new project and you give it a clear name.

The free version works for a beginner because it gives you the full Edit page without any payment. You learn the core tools first and you ignore the paid features until you truly need them. You save the project in one folder with your video clips so you can find everything later.

Ayesha from Lahore shows this idea in action with a free download on her laptop. She installs the program in the evening and she opens a new project for her study vlog. She saves the project in a folder named first video and she opens the same folder every day.

Learn the Edit Page and Import Your Clips

You open the Edit page when you follow a DaVinci Resolve Edit page tutorial in the program itself. You see the media pool on the left, the preview in the centre, and the timeline at the bottom. You drag your clips into the media pool and you check that each clip plays well before you cut anything.

This layout works because each part of the page has one clear job for your edit. You find clips fast in the media pool and you watch the result in the preview. You keep the timeline clean and you add clips in the order that tells your story best.

Bilal from Karachi shows this idea in action with a short travel video from his phone. He imports five clips into the media pool and he watches each one in the preview. He drags the best three clips to the timeline and he deletes the two clips that look shaky.

Cut Clips and Arrange Them on the Timeline

You cut clips on the timeline when you learn how to edit videos in DaVinci Resolve for beginners through DaVinci Resolve timeline basics. You place the playhead where the clip should end and you press the blade tool to split it. You delete the weak part and you join the strong parts so the story flows well.

Cutting works because short clips keep the viewer watching to the end of the video. You remove pauses, mistakes, and dull moments that add nothing to the story. You watch the full timeline once and you fix any jump that feels rough or sudden.

Sana from Islamabad shows this idea in action with a cooking video that runs too long. She cuts the waiting parts and she keeps only the steps that show the recipe clearly. She watches the full video once and her family enjoys the short version much more.

Add Text Music and Simple Colour Fixes

You add text and music after the cuts feel right and you keep both simple for your first video. You add one title at the start and one short label where a new step begins. You lower the music volume under your voice so viewers hear every word clearly.

Simple additions work because they guide the viewer without hiding the video itself. You fix dull colours with one gentle lift in brightness and warmth. You avoid many effects at once and you let the clean cut carry the video.

Usman from Faisalabad shows this idea in action with a shop tour video for his uncle store. He adds a simple title at the start and he lowers the music under his voice. He lifts the brightness a little and his shop looks bright and welcoming in the final video.

Export Your Video and Share It With Confidence

You export video from DaVinci Resolve by opening the Deliver page and choosing a simple preset for YouTube or a phone. You pick a clear file name and you save the video in the same folder as your project. You wait for the render to finish and you watch the full video once before you share it.

A simple preset works because it balances clear quality with a file size that uploads fast. You avoid custom settings at the start and you trust the preset that matches your platform. You check the sound on headphones and the picture on your phone before you publish the video.

Hira from Rawalpindi shows this idea in action with her first study vlog that she exports in one evening. She chooses the YouTube preset and she watches the full video on her phone.
