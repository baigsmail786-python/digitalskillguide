How Beginners Cut a Long Video into Short Clips in CapCut for Reels and Shorts

Digitalskillguide · Video Editing · 28 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You learn how to cut a long video into short clips in CapCut for beginners by marking the best moments first, splitting the video at those marks, and adding captions to each clip. You export each clip in a tall shape, and you post it as a Reel or a Short.

Most beginners cut a long video at random spots, and the clips lose viewers in seconds. They then post all the clips at once, and the channel gains nothing from the effort.

You will find the best moments, and you will split them into short clips. You will post five clips a week from one video.

Find the Best Moments in the Long Video First

You learn how to cut a long video into short clips in CapCut for beginners fastest when you find the best moments before you open the editor. A teacher in Lahore marks five moments in a forty minute lesson, and each mark becomes one clip that stands on its own. You watch the long video once, and you note the times when the speaker makes a clear point or the crowd reacts.

Watch Once and Mark the Strong Parts

You watch the long video once from start to finish, and you write down the time of every strong moment as you watch. You look for a clear tip, a funny line, a story, or a result that the viewer can enjoy in under a minute. You mark at least five moments, and you rank them by how well each one stands alone.

Pick Clips That Stand Alone Without Context

You pick clips that stand alone without context, because a Reel viewer never saw the full video and never will. You choose a moment that opens with the point itself, and you cut any intro that only makes sense inside the long video. You test each clip by watching it alone, and you keep the clips that pass the test.

Split the Video Into Clips in CapCut on Your Phone

You split the video into clips in CapCut on your phone, because the phone app carries the same split tool as the desktop version. A vlogger in Karachi splits a twenty minute video into five clips on her phone, and the whole job takes less than an hour. You import the long video, and you move the playhead to the first mark you wrote down.

Use the Split Tool at Each Mark

You use the split tool at each mark by tapping the timeline at the time you wrote down and choosing Split. You split at the start of the strong moment first, and you split again at the end to free the clip. You delete the parts you do not need, and you keep only the clip that carries the point.

Trim the Start and the End of Every Clip

You trim the start and the end of every clip, because the first second decides whether the viewer stays or swipes away. You cut the pause before the speaker starts, and you cut the trailing words after the point lands. You read How Beginners Cut Pauses and Mistakes Out of Videos in CapCut to clean the pauses inside each clip, and you save the clip before you move on.

Add Captions and a Hook to Each Short Clip

You add captions and a hook to each short clip, because most viewers watch Reels on mute and read the words instead. A creator in Islamabad added captions to her clips, and her watch time grew because viewers stayed to read the point. You open each clip with the strongest line, and you place the captions where the eye lands first.

Add Captions That People Can Read on Mute

You add captions to short video clips in CapCut for beginners by opening the Text menu and choosing Auto Captions for the clip. You check each line after the tool writes it, and you fix any word the tool heard wrong. You watch the clip on mute once, and you keep the captions only when you can follow the point by reading.

Open Each Clip With the Strongest Line

You open each clip with the strongest line, because the first words decide whether the viewer stays or swipes away. You move the best sentence to the start, and you cut the slow greeting that used to open the clip. You say the benefit in the first three seconds, and the viewer knows at once why the clip matters.

Export the Clips and Follow a One Hour Weekly Routine

You learn how to cut a long video into short clips in CapCut for beginners best when you export each clip right and follow a weekly routine. A creator in Peshawar follows a one hour routine each Sunday, and she posts five clips a week from one long video. You export each clip in a tall shape at 1080p, and you name the file with the day you will post it.

Export Each Clip for Reels and Shorts

You export short clips from CapCut for Reels and Shorts for beginners by tapping Export and choosing 1080p at 30 frames per second. You pick the tall shape before you export, and you check that the captions sit inside the frame. You upload only the clean version, and you read How Beginners Change Video Speed in CapCut for Better Videos to speed up slow parts before you export.

Cut Five Clips From One Video Every Week

You cut five clips from one video every week, and you learn how to cut a long video into short clips in CapCut for beginners by repeating the same routine each Sunday. You spend twenty minutes marking the best moments, twenty minutes splitting and trimming, and twenty minutes adding captions. You export the five clips in one batch, and you schedule one clip for each weekday.

A short clip for Reels and Shorts should run between thirty and sixty seconds, because viewers decide in the first seconds whether to stay.
