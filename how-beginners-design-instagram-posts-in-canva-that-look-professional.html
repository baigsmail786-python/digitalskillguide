How Beginners Design Instagram Posts in Canva That Look Professional

Graphic Design · 12 November 2026 · 7 min read

Design Instagram posts in Canva that look professional with the right size, fonts, colours, and layouts. This beginner guide shows each step clearly.

You make posts that look professional when you learn how to design Instagram posts in Canva with the right size and layouts. This guide shows you fonts, colours, templates, and carousels. You pick one style, you design with care, and you publish posts that earn saves.

Most beginners crowd a post with many fonts and they fill every corner with stickers. They change style each time and they give followers no reason to recognise their posts.

You finish this guide with a repeatable design process and a style you reuse. You design faster each time and your feed looks clean and consistent.

Set the Right Size and Layout First

You start when you learn how to design Instagram posts in Canva by setting the right size before you add any text. You check Instagram post size in Canva in the template settings and you use the full canvas without cropping later. You keep the main message inside the safe centre and you leave clean space around the edges.

Next you pick a simple layout with one focal point and you place the main words where the eye lands first. You leave white space around the text and you align every element with care. This clean layout helps the eye find the message fast and it keeps the post readable on a small phone screen.

Maryam from Multan shows this idea in action with a small food page. She sets the correct size first and she places the dish name in the centre of the canvas. She leaves clean space for a short benefit line and her posts look sharp on phones and laptops alike.

Choose Fonts and Colours That Match Your Brand

You choose Canva fonts and colours for Instagram by picking two fonts and three colours that match your topic. You use one bold font for headings and one simple font for short lines. You use the same colours in every post and you let followers recognise your style at a glance.

After that you test the colours at phone size and you check that the text stays clear against the background. You use dark text on light areas and light text on dark areas. You change any colour that feels dull and you keep only the colours that look clean and warm.

Ahmed from Peshawar shows this idea in action with a travel tips page. He picks two fonts and three colours that match his photos from the northern areas. He uses the same style in every post and his followers spot his posts in a crowded feed at once.

Use Templates Without Making Every Post Look the Same

You save time when you learn how to design Instagram posts in Canva with templates that you change for each new topic. You use Canva Instagram post templates for beginners as a starting point and you change the photo, words, and colours each time. You keep the layout and the fonts the same so the feed stays consistent.

This method works because a template gives you speed and your changes give you variety. You swap the main image and you rewrite the headline for the new tip. You keep the brand colours in place and you add one fresh element that fits the topic of the day.

Fatima from Sialkot shows this idea in action with a study tips page. She uses one template for tips and she changes the photo and headline for each new lesson. Her feed looks consistent and her followers read the new tip in seconds without confusion.

Design Carousels That People Swipe Through

You build Instagram carousel design in Canva by planning a clear story across five or six slides. You put the main promise on the first slide and you give one tip on each later slide. You end with a simple call to follow for the next carousel in the series.

Next you keep the same layout on every slide and you change only the words and one image. You add small numbers to show progress and you keep each slide short enough to read in seconds. This steady flow pulls the reader forward and it earns more saves than a single image post.

Zainab from Gujranwala shows this idea in action with a beauty tips page. She designs a carousel with one tip on each slide and she keeps the same colours throughout. Her followers swipe to the last slide and they save the carousel to read again later.

Export Check and Improve Your Next Post

You finish when you learn how to design Instagram posts in Canva by exporting the file in high quality and checking it before you publish. You export the post as a clear image and you preview it on your phone beside your other posts. You check the text, the colours, and the layout at phone size and you fix any weak part.

After that you compare the new post with your last three posts and you note the one that earns the most saves. You keep the winning style and you change only one element in the next design. This steady testing habit improves your posts with every design you publish.

Daniyal from Hyderabad shows this idea in action with a tech tips page. He exports each post in high quality and he previews it on his phone before upload. He keeps the style that wins saves and he changes one colour or word in each new post.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

What size should an Instagram post be in Canva?

You use the Instagram post preset in Canva and you design on the full canvas without cropping. You keep the main message in the centre and you leave clean space at the edges. You export in high quality and you preview the result at phone size before you post.

How many fonts should a beginner use in one post?

You use two fonts in one post and you keep the same pair in every design. You use a bold font for the headline and a simple font for short lines. You test the pair at phone size and you change it only when the text feels hard to read.

Do beginners need paid Canva to design good Instagram posts?

You learn how to design Instagram posts in Canva with the free plan and you need no paid upgrade at the start. You use free templates, free fonts, and your own photos for every design. You pay later only when you want premium photos or brand tools every week.

Which colours work best for Instagram posts?

You pick three colours that contrast well and you match one colour to your photo. You use light text on dark areas and dark text on light areas. You test the post beside other posts and you keep the colours that stand out without hurting the eyes.

How do beginners keep a feed looking consistent?

You keep one layout, two fonts, and three colours across all your posts. You change the photo and headline for each new topic and you keep the rest the same. You preview new posts beside old posts and you fix any design that breaks the pattern.

Should every Instagram post use a template?

You use a template for speed and you change it enough to fit each new topic. You keep the layout and fonts the same so followers recognise your posts fast. You design from a blank canvas only when a new idea needs a fresh layout.

How do beginners pick a good photo for a post?

You pick a photo that shows the result or the emotion of the post in one clear shot. You choose a bright image with a clean background and you remove clutter from the frame. You test the photo at small size and you keep it only when the subject stays clear.

How often should beginners change their post style?

You keep one style for many posts and you change only one element at a time. You compare the saves and shares and you keep the change that wins. You redesign the full style only when the numbers fall for many posts in a row.

You set the right size and you build a clean layout around one clear message. You choose fonts and colours that match your brand and you use templates with fresh changes each time. You design carousels that pull readers forward and you check each post so the next design looks even better.
