How Beginners Change Video Speed in CapCut for Better Videos

Digitalskillguide · Video Editing · 25 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You learn how to change video speed in CapCut for beginners by selecting the clip, opening Speed, and moving the slider above one for faster or below one for slower. Normal suits a whole clip, and Curve suits a smooth ramp inside one clip.

Most beginners speed up the whole video, and the result feels rushed from start to finish. They then lose viewers in the first seconds, when only the dull part needed speed.

You will speed up the dull parts, slow down the key moments, and match the pace to the story. You will then export a video that feels smooth from start to finish.

Open Speed and Set a Simple Multiplier

You open Speed by tapping the clip in the timeline and choosing Speed in the toolbar, and CapCut shows you two choices: Normal and Curve. You pick Normal when you want one steady speed for the whole clip, and you move the slider above one to speed up or below one to slow down. You preview the result before you export it, and you adjust the number until the pace feels right.

Speed Up a Whole Clip With Normal

You tap the clip, open Speed, and choose Normal, and you drag the slider to the right for a faster pace. You use one and a half times for a gentle lift, and you use two times when the clip needs real energy. You tap the check mark to apply the change, and the clip plays faster at once.

Slow Down a Clip for a Key Moment

You drag the slider to the left when you want slow motion, and you use half speed for a clear dramatic pause. You slow down only the moment that matters, such as a goal, a reaction, or a reveal, and you keep the rest at normal speed. You check that the slow part stays smooth, and you trim it short when it starts to drag.

Speed Up Only Part of a Clip With Split

You learn how to change video speed in CapCut for beginners best when you split a clip and change only one part of it. You move the playhead to the point where the faster section starts, and you tap Split to cut the clip there. You move the playhead to the point where the section ends, and you tap Split again to free the middle piece.

Isolate the Section You Want to Change

You select the middle segment after the second split, and you check that the playhead sits exactly on the cut. You open Speed on that segment alone, and the rest of the clip keeps its original pace. You set the speed for the segment, and you preview the join on both sides so the cut feels smooth. You undo the split at once when the cut sits in the wrong place, and you try again with the playhead moved a little.

Keep the Audio in Step With the Picture

You remember that speeding up a clip speeds up its audio too, and voices turn high and thin when the speed climbs. You look for the pitch option in the Speed menu, and you turn it on so CapCut keeps the voice natural. You mute the clip audio when the sound does not matter, and you add music that matches the new pace instead.

Build a Speed Ramp With Curve

You build a speed ramp with Curve when you want the pace to change gradually inside one clip, and the ramp feels far smoother than a sudden jump. You select the clip, open Speed, and choose Curve, and CapCut shows you presets such as Montage and Hero. You pick a preset that matches the mood, and you preview the changing speeds before you apply it.

Pick a Preset or Draw Your Own Curve

You pick a preset when it matches the effect you want, and you tap Custom when you want full control. You drag the curve points upward to speed up and downward to slow down, and you drag them sideways to decide when each change happens. You keep the curve simple at first, with one slow part and one fast part, and you add more points only when the simple curve feels right. You preview the ramp twice, and you smooth any point that jerks the motion.

Match the Ramp to the Music Beat

You match the ramp to the music beat when the video carries a song, and the speed change lands on the beat instead of fighting it. You place the fastest point on the strongest beat, and you let the slow part sit on the quiet stretch. You add a transition at the point where the speed changes, and the cut hides the join.

Choose the Right Speed for Each Moment

You choose the right speed for each moment, because one speed never fits a whole video. You speed up the intro when it drags, and you keep the tutorial steps at normal speed so viewers can follow them. You slow down the ending when you want the viewer to feel the result, and you speed up the travel shots that add nothing to the story. Speed Up Intros and Travel Shots

You speed up the intro when the first seconds feel slow, and you use one and a half times so the energy rises without losing words. You speed up travel shots and waiting shots to two times, because the viewer gains nothing from watching a long walk at full speed. You keep the speed steady inside each segment, and you cut back to normal speed before the next key line. You preview the intro once, and you trim it further when it still feels long.

Slow Down Reveals and Reactions

You slow down a reveal when the viewer needs a second to feel it, and you use half speed so the moment lands with weight. You slow down a reaction for the same reason, and you keep the slow part short so it never drags. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I speed up a video in CapCut on my phone?

You open CapCut, tap New project, and select your video. You tap the clip in the timeline, choose Speed and then Normal, and move the slider above one. You preview the result, and you tap Export when the pacing feels right.

How do I slow down a video in CapCut?

You select the clip, open Speed, and choose Normal, and you move the slider below one. You use half speed for a clear slow motion effect, and you keep the slow part short so it never drags. You preview the clip before you export it.

What is the difference between Normal and Curve in CapCut?

Normal sets one steady speed for the whole clip, and Curve changes the speed gradually inside the clip. You use Normal for a simple faster or slower clip, and you use Curve for a smooth ramp such as a slow build into a fast finish. You pick a preset in Curve, or you draw your own curve with Custom.

How do I speed up only part of a video in CapCut?

You move the playhead to the start of the section and tap Split, and you move it to the end and tap Split again. You select the middle segment, and you open Speed on that segment alone. You set the speed for the segment, and the rest of the clip keeps its original pace.

Why does my audio sound strange after I change the speed?

Speeding up a clip speeds up its audio too, and voices turn high and thin when the speed climbs. You turn on the pitch option in the Speed menu so CapCut keeps the voice natural. You mute the clip audio when the sound does not matter, and you add music that matches the new pace.

What speed should I use for a tutorial video?

You keep tutorial steps at normal speed so viewers can follow each move, and you speed up the setup shots that add nothing to the lesson. You use one and a half times for a gentle lift, and you slow down the key step so the viewer sees it clearly. You preview the whole video before you export it.

Can I change video speed in CapCut on desktop?

You can change video speed in CapCut on desktop, and the steps match the phone version. You click the clip, open Speed, and choose Standard for a steady speed or Curve for a ramp. You enter the speed number or drag the slider, and you click Export when the pacing feels right.

How do I learn to change video speed in CapCut as a beginner?

You learn how to change video speed in CapCut for beginners by starting with Normal speed on a whole clip. You then split a clip and change one part, and you try Curve once the split feels easy. You finish by matching the speed to each moment, and the habit turns into a skill.
