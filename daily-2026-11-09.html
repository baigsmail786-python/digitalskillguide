After this guide you will run your own repository with confidence. You will commit changes, open branches and send pull requests. You will finish with a live project link you can show to anyone.

Learn the Core Ideas: Repositories, Commits and Branches

How Freelancers Write a Contract That Protects Their Payment

Category: Freelancing • Keyword: how to write a freelance contract

Write a freelance contract that protects your payment: name the work, set fair payment terms, limit revisions and get it signed before you start work.

A freelance contract protects your payment by putting the work, the price and the deadline in writing. You and your client sign it before any work starts. This guide shows you how to write a freelance contract that keeps you safe.

Most freelancers start work on a chat message and a verbal promise. The client adds new tasks, delays payment and the freelancer has no proof. They lose money because they never wrote the deal down.

After this guide you will write a contract for every project. You will set clear payment terms and clear limits on revisions. You will work with confidence because the deal sits in writing.

Name the Work, the Price and the Deadline in Plain Words

You start the contract by naming the work in plain words. You list each deliverable, such as three web pages or ten social posts. You write the total price and the deadline next to the work. You learn how to write a freelance contract by stating the deal clearly on page one.

You describe the work so clearly that a stranger could read it and understand it. You name the file types, the sizes and the platforms involved. You leave out vague words that let a client add new tasks. You read the work section aloud and you fix any line that sounds unclear.

A designer in Lahore writes a contract for a shop logo and a price list. She names the logo, the colour files and the delivery date in the first lines. She lists the total price and the payment dates beside the work. You name your work the same way and your client will know exactly what they buy.

Set Payment Terms That Protect Your Cash Flow

You set payment terms that split the fee into two or three parts. You ask for an advance of thirty to fifty percent before you start. You link the next payment to a clear milestone such as a first draft. You take the final payment before you hand over the final files.

You name the payment method in the contract, such as bank transfer or JazzCash. You write the number of days the client has to pay each invoice. You add a small late fee for payments that arrive after the due date. You keep a copy of every invoice and every payment receipt.

A writer in Karachi asks for half the fee before she starts an article pack. She sends the first draft after the advance clears. She delivers the final files only after the last payment arrives. You set payment terms the same way and your cash flow will stay steady.

Define Revisions So Scope Creep Stops Early

You define revisions in the contract so extra work costs extra money. You offer two rounds of revisions inside the quoted price. You learn how to write a freelance contract that stops scope creep by naming the limit in plain words.

You write that any change after the second round counts as new work. You name your hourly rate for extra changes in the same clause. You ask the client to send all revision notes in one message. You confirm the notes in writing before you start the next round.

A video editor in Faisalabad offers two revision rounds in every contract. A client asks for a third round with new scenes and he quotes his hourly rate. The client accepts the extra fee and the project stays profitable. You define revisions the same way and scope creep will stop early.

Add a Simple Clause for Late Payment and Cancellation

You add a clause that says what happens when payment arrives late. You name a small fee for each week of delay. You write that you pause work when payment stays unpaid for fourteen days. You keep the clause short so both sides understand it.

You add a cancellation clause that protects both you and the client. You write that the client pays for the work done so far when they cancel. You write that you return the advance only when you cancel the project. You keep the terms fair so clients sign without fear.

A developer in Multan adds a late fee clause to every contract after one bad project. A client pays ten days late and the fee covers his lost time. He pauses the next phase until the payment clears. You add the same clauses and late payments will hurt you less.

Get the Contract Signed Before You Start Any Work

You send the contract to the client and you ask for a signed copy before you start. You accept a digital signature or a clear photo of a signed page. You save the signed copy in a folder with the project name. You never start work on a promise alone.

You read the signed contract once more and you check the names, the price and the dates. You confirm the start date and the delivery date with the client in chat. You keep the contract where you can open it fast during the project. You refer to it calmly when a dispute starts.

A freelancer in Islamabad sends a one page contract before every project. Her client signs it on WhatsApp the same day and she starts work the next morning. She has never chased a payment without a contract since. You get every contract signed first and your work will feel safe.
