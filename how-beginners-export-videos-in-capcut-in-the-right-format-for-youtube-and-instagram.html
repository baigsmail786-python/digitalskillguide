How Beginners Export Videos in CapCut in the Right Format for YouTube and Instagram

Digitalskillguide · Video Editing · 27 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You learn how to export videos in CapCut for YouTube and Instagram for beginners by picking the right aspect ratio, resolution, and frame rate. You set 16:9 for YouTube and 9:16 for Shorts and Reels, and you check the file before you upload it.

Most beginners export every video with the same settings, and the video comes out blurry or cropped on one platform. They then upload it anyway, and viewers leave in the first seconds.

You will export for YouTube, Shorts, and Reels with the right settings for each one. You will then keep a settings card that makes the next export take less than a minute.

Pick the Right Aspect Ratio Before You Export

You pick the right aspect ratio before you export, because the ratio decides whether the video fills the screen or leaves black bars. You use a wide shape for YouTube, and you use a tall shape for Shorts and Reels. A beginner in Karachi set the ratio first for her travel video, and the video filled the screen on both platforms.

Use 16:9 for YouTube and 9:16 for Shorts and Reels

You use 16:9 for a standard YouTube video, because the wide shape fits a laptop and a television screen. You use 9:16 for a Short, a Reel, or a TikTok, because the tall shape fills a phone screen from top to bottom. You never export a wide video as a tall one, and you never stretch a tall clip to fit a wide frame. You check the ratio in the project settings, and you fix it before you add the first clip.

Set the Ratio at the Start of the Project

You set the ratio when you create the project, and you pick the shape that matches where the video will play. You tap the ratio button in CapCut, and you choose 16:9 or 9:16 from the list. You start again with the right ratio when the preview looks wrong, and you save yourself a second export later.

Choose Resolution Frame Rate and Format for Each Platform

You learn how to export videos in CapCut for YouTube and Instagram for beginners fastest when you choose resolution, frame rate, and format for each platform. You pick 1080p for most videos, and you save 4K for footage that truly needs it. A vlogger in Islamabad exports this way, and his videos stay sharp on both YouTube and Instagram.

Pick 1080p for Most Videos and 4K Only When You Need It

You pick 1080p for most videos, because it looks sharp on a phone and a laptop without a huge file. You pick 4K only when you filmed in 4K and you want to crop or zoom without losing detail. You check the file size after the export, and you drop back to 1080p when the file feels too large to upload. You keep the same resolution across a series, and your channel gains a steady look.

Pick the Frame Rate That Matches Your Footage

You pick the frame rate that matches your footage, because a mismatch makes the video look jumpy. You use 30 frames per second for most talking videos, and you use 60 frames per second for fast action or sport. You preview the export once, and you watch the motion for any stutter before you upload.

Remove the Ending Logo and Check the File Before You Upload

You remove the ending logo and check the file before you upload, because a logo at the end tells viewers the video came from a free tool. You delete the ending clip from the timeline, and you watch the last two seconds to confirm it is gone. You play the exported file once on your phone, and you check the picture, the sound, and the text. A student in Lahore caught a missing caption this way, and she fixed it before her viewers ever saw it.

Delete the CapCut Ending Clip

You delete the CapCut ending clip by tapping it on the timeline and choosing delete. You check the end of the timeline after you delete it, and you confirm that your own last clip now ends the video. You add your own ending instead, such as a short message or a subscribe prompt, and the video feels like yours. You export only after the ending looks clean, and you never leave the default clip in place.

Watch the Exported File Once Before You Upload

You learn how to export videos in CapCut for YouTube and Instagram for beginners best when you watch the exported file once before you upload it. You play it on your phone, and you check the picture for blur, the sound for gaps, and the text for spelling. You check the first three seconds last, because viewers decide in that moment whether to stay. You export the file again when you spot a fault, and you upload only the clean version.

Use a Settings Card for YouTube Shorts Reels and TikTok

You use a settings card for YouTube, Shorts, Reels, and TikTok now, because one card on a single page stops the guesswork at export time. You write the ratio, resolution, and frame rate for each platform on the card, and you keep the card beside your desk. A creator in Peshawar keeps this card on her wall, and her exports now match each platform on the first try.

Keep a Settings Card for Each Platform

You keep a settings card for each platform, and you write the ratio, resolution, frame rate, and format on one line each. You write YouTube on the first line, Shorts on the second, Reels on the third, and TikTok on the fourth. You update the card when a platform changes its advice, and you keep the old card until the new one feels right. Export One Test Clip and Compare the Results

You export one test clip first, and you watch it on YouTube and on Instagram before you export the full video. You compare the sharpness, the framing, and the sound on both platforms, and you note any difference. You change one setting at a time, and you export again to see if the change helped.
