Freelancing After a Course: How to Win Your First Orders on Fiverr & Upwork

All levels · 10 min read · Oct 2026

Finishing a course is step one. Getting paid is step two — and it is a separate skill. Profiles, gigs, proposals and pricing, explained plainly.

Every week, skilled people finish good courses and then earn nothing — not because their skill is weak, but because nobody taught them the second skill: selling the first one. Freelancing platforms are marketplaces, and marketplaces have rules. Learn them and your course investment starts paying back.

Your profile is a shop window

Buyers decide in seconds. Your profile photo, title and first two lines must answer one question: “What result will I get?” Write “I design logos that make small brands look established”, not “I am a graphic designer”. Add your course projects as portfolio samples from day one — they are proof, even before your first order.

Gigs and proposals that convert

  • Offer one narrow service per gig — “YouTube thumbnails” beats “graphic design”.
  • Price a small entry package so a first buyer risks little.
  • In proposals, talk about the client's project first, your skills second.
  • Answer messages fast; response time is shown publicly on your profile.
  • Deliver slightly more than promised — reviews are your real marketing.

Pricing without fear

Start lower than your target rate for your first few orders to collect reviews, then raise prices every few completed jobs. Never compete on price alone for long — compete on clarity, speed and reliability, which is what repeat buyers actually pay for.

Tip: Treat your first month as paid practice, not a verdict on your talent. Almost every successful freelancer's first orders were small, underpriced and essential.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Why am I not getting orders after finishing my course?

Usually because selling is a separate skill from the course skill. Check your profile title, your first two lines, how narrow your gig is and whether you show sample work — buyers decide in seconds, before they read your full description.

Should I lower my prices to win my first orders?

You can price a small entry package fairly low for your first few orders to collect reviews, but do not compete on price alone for long. Raise prices gradually as reviews grow, and compete on clarity, speed and reliability.

What makes a Fiverr gig or Upwork proposal convert?

A narrow service, a title that states the result the buyer gets, relevant samples, and a proposal that talks about the client's project first and your skills second. Fast replies also help, because response time is shown on your profile.
