How Beginners Design an Infographic in Canva That People Actually Read

Graphic Design · 14 November 2026 · 7 min read

Make an infographic in Canva as a beginner. This guide covers size, templates, charts, icons, and export for clear reading.

You make an infographic in Canva by picking one message, choosing a tall template, and adding short text with clear charts. You learn how to make an infographic in Canva for beginners with a simple layout that readers follow from top to bottom. You need only a free Canva account.

Most beginners crowd the page with long paragraphs and they use many colours that fight each other. They add charts with no labels and they leave readers confused.

You finish this guide with a clean infographic that readers scan in seconds and share with friends. You gain the skill to turn any topic into a clear visual story.

Pick One Message and Plan the Layout

You pick one message when you learn how to make an infographic in Canva for beginners with a clear plan. You write the main point in one short line and you list three facts that support it. You sketch the layout on paper and you place the title at the top and the facts below it.

One message works because readers grasp the point in seconds and they keep reading. You avoid a page that mixes many topics and tires the reader. You keep each fact short and you give each one its own space on the page.

Ayesha from Lahore shows this idea in action with an infographic about healthy breakfast foods. She writes one main point about protein and she lists three foods with short facts. She sketches the layout on paper and her readers follow it from top to bottom with ease.

Set the Right Size and Choose a Template

You set the infographic size in Canva first when you learn how to make an infographic in Canva for beginners with the right frame. You open a blank design and you pick a tall size that fits phones and social feeds. You choose Canva infographic templates for beginners and you pick one with a clean layout.

A tall size works because readers scroll down on phones and they read one block at a time. You keep the width narrow and you let the content flow in a clear order. You change the template colours to match your topic and you keep the same fonts throughout.

Bilal from Karachi shows this idea in action with a tall template for a cricket stats infographic. He picks a clean template and he changes the colours to green and white. He checks the design on his phone and every block reads well in one scroll.

Add Charts and Icons That Explain the Data

You add infographic charts and icons in Canva when you learn how to make an infographic in Canva for beginners with clear visuals. You pick one chart for each fact and you label every number in plain words. You add one icon beside each point so readers grasp the idea at a glance.

Charts work because readers see a comparison faster than they read a paragraph. You keep each chart simple and you show only the numbers that support your point. You use the same icon style throughout so the page feels calm and clear.

Sana from Islamabad shows this idea in action with a chart about study hours for her class page. She draws one bar chart with three bars and she labels each bar with a clear number. Her classmates read the chart in seconds and they share the infographic in their group.

Write Short Text That People Actually Read

You write short text for each block and you keep every line under ten words. You use plain words that a new reader grasps at once. You place the most useful fact first and you leave out details that add no value.

Short text works because readers scan an infographic instead of reading it like a book. You earn their attention with clear lines and you lose it with long paragraphs. You read each line aloud and you cut any word that adds no meaning.

Usman from Faisalabad shows this idea in action with an infographic about tailoring prices for his shop. He writes each price in one short line and he adds one icon for each service. His customers read the prices in seconds and they ask for the service they want.

Export the Infographic and Share It Well

You export infographic from Canva by choosing the PNG option for a clear image. You download the file and you open it on your phone to check every word. You share the image on your page and you add a short caption that names the main point.

A clear export works because a sharp image reads well on every screen and in every feed. You avoid a blurry file that hides your text and weakens your message. You keep the original Canva file so you can fix a spelling slip and export again in minutes.

Hira from Rawalpindi shows this idea in action with an infographic about mehndi designs for her page. She exports the file as a PNG and she shares it with a short caption.
