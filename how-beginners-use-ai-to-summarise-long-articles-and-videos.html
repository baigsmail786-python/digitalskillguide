How Beginners Use AI to Summarise Long Articles and Videos

Digitalskillguide · AI · 26 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You learn how to use AI to summarise articles and videos for beginners by pasting the text or the link into an AI tool. You tell the tool the length you want and the points you care about most. The tool returns the main ideas in plain words.

Most beginners paste a long article with no length and no focus, and the tool returns a vague paragraph. They then trust it without reading the source.

You will summarise one article and one video in ten minutes, and you will check each summary against the source. You will then keep a routine you can repeat every morning.

Pick the Right Tool for the Job

You learn how to use AI to summarise articles and videos for beginners fastest when you pick one tool and learn it well. ChatGPT and Claude handle pasted text with ease, and both give you a summary in seconds. YouTube tools and NotebookLM handle video links by reading the transcript first. A student in Multan uses NotebookLM for her lectures, and she turns a one hour talk into a one page summary before class.

Summarise an Article With ChatGPT or Claude

You open ChatGPT or Claude, and you paste the article text into the chat box. You write a clear request that names the length and the focus, such as five short points on the main argument. You read the summary once, and you compare each point with the article before you trust it. You ask the tool to shorten the summary further when it runs too long, and you save the final version in your notes.

Summarise a Video From Its Transcript

You copy the video link, and you paste it into a tool that reads transcripts, such as NotebookLM or a YouTube summary tool. The tool reads the spoken words first, and it then turns them into a short summary of the main points. You watch the first two minutes yourself, and you check that the summary matches what the speaker actually said. You ask for timestamps when you want to jump back to one part, and you save the link with the summary.

Write a Prompt That Gets a Useful Summary

You write a good prompt before you paste anything, because a clear request gives you a clear summary. You name the length first, such as five short points or one short paragraph. You name the focus next, such as the main argument, the steps, or the conclusion. A freelancer in Karachi writes her prompts this way for client reports, and she finishes in minutes a task that used to take an hour.

Name the Length and the Focus

You tell the tool the length in plain words, such as five short points or one short paragraph. You tell it the focus too, such as the steps, the argument, or the result. Read How Beginners Use ChatGPT to Learn a New Skill With Better Prompts for prompt patterns that work across every AI tool. You test the same prompt on two articles, and you keep the version that gives you cleaner summaries.

Ask for Plain Words and Real Quotes

You learn how to use AI to summarise articles and videos for beginners best when you ask for plain words and one real quote from the source. You add a line that asks the tool to keep names, dates, and numbers exactly as the source states them. You read the quote in the source yourself, and you fix it when the tool changes a word. You keep the summary honest this way, and your notes stay safe to share.

Check Every Summary Before You Trust It

You check every summary against the source, because AI tools sometimes invent a detail or mix up two facts. You read the source headings first, and you compare each summary point with the part it came from. You treat a summary as a map, and you walk the ground yourself before you quote it. A teacher in Peshawar caught a wrong date this way, and she fixed it before she shared the notes with her class.

Compare Each Point With the Source

You open the source beside the summary, and you match each point to the part it came from. You mark any point you cannot find, and you ask the tool to show you where it came from. You delete a point when the source never says it, and you rewrite it in your own words when the wording feels off. You keep this check short, and it takes less than two minutes for most summaries.

Watch for Invented Names and Numbers

You read names, dates, and numbers twice, because AI tools invent these details more than any other. You compare each number with the source, and you delete it when the source shows a different one. You check quoted words letter by letter, and you fix any quote the tool changed. You never share a summary until the names and numbers match the source.

Build a Ten Minute Morning Routine

You build a ten minute routine now, because a routine turns a trick into a habit you keep. You pick one article and one video each morning, and you summarise both before you start work. You save each summary in one note, and you review the note at the end of the week. A student in Lahore follows this routine before class, and she walks in knowing the reading without staying up late.

Run the Routine in Four Small Steps

You start by picking the article and the video, and you open both in two tabs. You paste each one into your tool, and you ask for five short points on each. You check both summaries against the sources, and you fix any point that looks wrong. You save the two summaries in one note, and you add the date so you can find them later.

Keep Your Summaries in One Place

You keep every summary in one note or one folder, and you give each entry a clear title and a date. You add the source link under each summary, and you can open the source again in one click. You read the note at the end of the week, and you pick the three ideas worth keeping. Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI summarise a long article well?

AI summarises a long article well when you paste the full text and name the length and the focus. You ask for five short points or one short paragraph, and you check each point against the source. You treat the summary as a starting point, and you read the full article when a point matters for your work.

Can AI summarise a YouTube video?

Which AI tool is best for summarising articles?

ChatGPT and Claude suit beginners best for articles, because you paste text into an ordinary chat and ask in plain words. NotebookLM suits videos and long documents, because it reads the source and shows you where each point came from. You pick one tool first, and you learn it well before you try another.

How do I write a good summary prompt?

You write a good summary prompt by naming the length, the focus, and the tone in two short sentences. You ask for five short points on the main argument, or one short paragraph in plain words. You test the prompt on two articles, and you keep the version that gives you cleaner summaries.

How do I know if an AI summary is wrong?

You know a summary is wrong when a point has no match in the source, or when a name, date, or number differs from the source. You open the source beside the summary, and you match each point to the part it came from. You delete any point the source never says, and you fix any number that does not match.

Can I trust an AI summary for study notes?

You can trust an AI summary for study notes after you check it against the source yourself. You compare each point with the source, and you fix names, dates, and numbers before you share the notes. You keep the source link with the summary, so you can open the source again in one click.

How long should an AI summary be?

An AI summary should run five short points or one short paragraph for most articles, and you ask for more only when the article carries many sections. You match the length to your purpose, and a revision note needs more detail than a quick catch up. You ask the tool to shorten the summary when it runs too long.

How do I learn to use AI for summaries as a beginner?

You start with one tool, and you add the check step before you trust any summary. You then keep the ten minute routine, and the habit saves you hours of reading each week.
