How Beginners Design a Social Media Post in Canva That Stops the Scroll

Digitalskillguide · Graphic Design · 25 November 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: You learn how to design a social media post in Canva for beginners by picking the right size, starting from a template, and changing the photo, the text, and the colours. The workflow takes about four minutes once you stop browsing templates.

Most beginners scroll through templates for an hour, and they never start the design. They then rush the post at the end, and it looks like every other post in the feed.

You will finish a clean post in four minutes, and you will repeat the same routine for every post. You will then build a small set of layouts that keep your page consistent.

Pick the Right Size Before You Touch a Template

You pick the right size first, because a post made at the wrong size gets cropped and loses its headline. You use a square canvas of 1080 by 1080 pixels for a feed post, a tall canvas of 1080 by 1920 pixels for a story, and a wide canvas for a cover image. You open Canva, and you type the platform name in the search box so Canva sets the size for you.

Use Square for the Feed and Tall for Stories

You use a square shape for a feed post, because it sits well on every screen and it crops cleanly. You use a tall shape for a story or a reel cover, because it fills the whole phone screen. You use a wide shape for a cover image, because it spans the top of the page. You check the size in the top bar before you add anything, and you fix it at once when it looks wrong.

Start From a Blank Canvas When Templates Distract You

You start from a blank canvas when templates pull you into endless browsing, and you add one photo and one headline yourself. You keep the layout simple, and you let the photo carry the post. You save the blank layout as your own starting point, and you reuse it for the next post. You return to templates only when you need a fresh idea, and you set a timer so browsing never eats your whole hour.

Use a Template Without Looking Like a Template

You learn how to design a social media post in Canva for beginners best when you treat a template as a starting point and not as a finished post. You search templates by your industry and not just by format, and you filter by style so the results match your brand. You pick one template, and you change the photo first, because your own photo does more than any other change to stop the template look.

Change the Photo First and the Text Second

You upload your own photo and drop it into the frame, and the post gains a face that no other page shares. You then rewrite the headline in your own words, and you keep it to twenty words or fewer so it reads in one glance. You change the colours to your brand colours, and you keep the same two colours on every post.

Keep Two Fonts and Two Colours on Every Post

You keep two fonts at most on a post, with one font for the headline and one font for the body text. You keep two colours at most, and you use the same pair on every post so your page builds a look people recognise. You set the headline large enough to read at a glance, and you keep the body text large enough to read on a small phone screen.

Write Short Text That People Read in One Glance

You write short text on the graphic, because people scroll fast and they read a post in one glance. You put the main point in the headline, and you keep the rest of the message in the caption below the post. You use plain words that a stranger understands, and you skip clever words that need a second read. Read How Beginners Design Instagram Carousels in Canva That People Swipe Through for a longer format that keeps readers swiping through your posts.

Put One Idea on Each Post

You put one idea on each post, and you let that idea own the whole graphic. You move the second idea to the next post, and your page gains a steady rhythm that followers trust. You ask one question in the caption when you want replies, and you answer each reply the same day. You check the post after a day, and you note which idea earned the most saves.

Leave Empty Space Around the Headline

You leave empty space around the headline, because crowded text tires the eye and pushes readers away. You keep a margin between the text and the edge of the canvas, and you let the photo breathe around the words. You remove one element whenever the design feels busy, and the post gains clarity with each removal. You preview the post at small size, and you fix any text that turns unreadable.

Export Cleanly and Build a Repeatable Routine

You export the post in the right format before you share it, because a blurry post tells readers you rushed it. You download a feed post as a PNG for crisp text, and you download a photo heavy post as a JPG for a smaller file. You name the file clearly, and you keep the design saved in Canva for the next version.

Download in the Right Format for Each Platform

You click Share and then Download, and you pick PNG when the post carries sharp text and JPG when it carries a large photo. You check the file size before you post, and you compress it when the platform refuses a large file. You keep the original design in Canva, and you make the next post from a copy of it. You never post a screenshot of your design, because it loses quality and looks unpolished.

Save the Layout as Your Own Template

You save the finished layout as your own template, and you reuse the same structure for the next post. You change only the photo, the headline, and the colours, and the post takes four minutes instead of forty.
