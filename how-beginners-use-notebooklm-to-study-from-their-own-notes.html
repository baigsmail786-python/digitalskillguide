How Beginners Use NotebookLM to Study From Their Own Notes

AI · 18 November 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: This guide shows you how to use NotebookLM for beginners with your own class notes as the source. You upload PDFs, slides, or a YouTube link, and NotebookLM answers only from those sources.

Most beginners paste a question into a chatbot and accept the first answer, even when the answer comes from general knowledge instead of their own notes. You avoid this trap by loading your own sources first and asking NotebookLM to answer only from them.

You finish with a study guide built from your own material, a set of practice questions, and an audio overview you can replay before the exam.

Add Your Own Notes as Sources First

NotebookLM answers from the sources you give it, so the quality of your sources decides the quality of its answers. You gather your class notes, your slides, and your textbook chapter before you ask a single question. This focus keeps every answer inside your course material, and it stops the tool from drifting into general facts.

Upload PDFs and slides from your class

Open a new notebook, and add the PDF of your textbook chapter and the slides from your last three lectures. A student in Karachi used this method for her biology exam, and she added the chapter PDF plus the lab handout before she asked anything. Her answers quoted her own notes, so she revised the exact facts her teacher had taught.

Add a YouTube link or pasted text

You can also add a YouTube link for a lecture video, and NotebookLM pulls the spoken words into the same source list. Paste short notes or a summary you wrote yourself when you have no file for a topic. This mix of sources covers the gaps in any single file, and it gives the tool more material to answer from.

Ask Questions That Target the Gaps in Your Understanding

Good questions turn NotebookLM from a search box into a tutor, and this guide shows you how to use NotebookLM for beginners with questions that target real gaps. You ask about the parts you find confusing, and you ask for plain words instead of textbook language. This habit builds real understanding, and it protects you from memorising an answer you never checked.

Ask for plain explanations of hard parts

Pick the hardest idea in your chapter, and ask NotebookLM to explain it as if you missed the lecture. The tool answers from your sources, and it uses the same terms your teacher used. A student who struggled with osmosis asked for a plain explanation with a daily life example, and the answer used a kitchen sponge to show how water moves.

Ask for comparisons and examples

Ask NotebookLM to compare two ideas side by side, such as mitosis and meiosis, and the tool lays out the differences in plain rows of facts. Comparisons expose the small details that exams love to test, and they stop you from mixing up similar terms. Turn Sources Into a Study Guide and a Quiz

NotebookLM can turn your sources into a study guide, and you should use that guide as a map rather than a script to memorise. You ask for the main ideas first, and you read them with your own notes open beside them. You mark the ideas you already know, and you circle the ones that still feel shaky.

Build a study guide from the main ideas

Ask NotebookLM for a study guide that lists the main ideas in the order your course teaches them. Read each idea and say it aloud in your own words before you move to the next one. A student who spoke each idea aloud found her weak spots in minutes, and she fixed them before the exam instead of after it.

Test yourself with generated questions

Ask NotebookLM to write ten practice questions from your sources, and answer them with your notes closed. This closed book test shows you what you truly know, and it beats rereading the chapter for the third time. Your second attempt should feel easier, because you studied the gaps instead of the whole chapter again.

Listen to the Audio Overview While You Review

The Audio Overview turns your sources into a short spoken discussion between two voices, and it gives your eyes a rest while your ears keep learning. You generate the overview after you upload the sources, and you listen to it once before you start detailed study. This first listen builds a big picture in your mind, and it makes the details easier to place later.

Use the audio for a second pass

Listen to the overview again the day before the exam, and treat it as a fast review of the whole topic. A student who listened during her morning walk recalled the main points in class the next day, and she answered questions her friends had missed. Note the points you still miss

Keep a small notebook beside you while the audio plays, and write down every point that still feels unclear. These notes become your final revision list, and they tell you exactly what to ask NotebookLM next. You close the loop by asking about each unclear point, and you listen to the new answer with fresh attention.

Your Next Step With NotebookLM

You now know the full loop, from sources to questions to a study guide and an audio review. This guide showed you how to use NotebookLM for beginners, and the same loop works for any subject that gives you notes to study. Pick one topic, finish the loop, and let the result shape how you study next time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NotebookLM?

NotebookLM is a research and study tool from Google that answers from the sources you upload. You add PDFs, slides, web links, or YouTube videos, and the tool builds answers only from that material. It works best when your sources match the topic you must learn.

Is NotebookLM free for beginners?

NotebookLM offers a free version that covers the core features in this guide, and this guide shows you how to use NotebookLM for beginners inside that free version. You can upload sources, ask questions, and generate an audio overview without paying. Paid plans add more sources and more features, so start free and upgrade only if you hit a limit.

How many sources can I add to a notebook?

The free version sets a limit on sources per notebook, and the exact limit can change, so check the current limit inside the tool before you plan a big upload. Start with the five or six sources that match one topic. Add more only when the topic truly needs them.

Can NotebookLM read my handwritten notes?

NotebookLM reads typed PDFs and text well, and it struggles with messy handwriting in photos. Type your handwritten notes or paste a clean summary instead. This typed version also helps you revise, because typing forces you to choose clear words.

How is NotebookLM different from ChatGPT for studying?

ChatGPT answers from broad training data, while NotebookLM answers only from the sources you upload. This difference matters for exams, because your teacher tests your course material and not general knowledge. Use ChatGPT for broad ideas, and use NotebookLM when the facts must come from your notes.

Can I trust NotebookLM answers without checking?

No, you should check every key fact against your own sources before you trust it. NotebookLM cites its sources, so click the citation and read the exact lines. This check takes seconds, and it catches the rare mistake before it reaches your exam.

What is the Audio Overview in NotebookLM?

The Audio Overview turns your sources into a spoken discussion between two voices. You can listen to it while you walk or rest your eyes. Treat it as a review, and check any surprising claim in your notes.

Can I share a NotebookLM notebook with classmates?

Yes, you can share a notebook with classmates so the group studies from the same sources. Each person can ask their own questions, and the group can build a shared study guide. Agree on the source list first, so the notebook stays focused on one topic.
